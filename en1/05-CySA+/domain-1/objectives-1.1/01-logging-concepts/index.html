DEFINITION

Logging Concepts

Logging is the process of recording events and activities occurring on systems, applications, network devices, and security tools for monitoring, investigation, troubleshooting, and incident response.

EVENT EXAMPLE
10:42:01 auth.login user=analyst status=success
10:42:08 firewall src=10.0.2.15 action=allow
10:42:11 endpoint process=powershell.exe
10:42:14 dns query=example.local
10:42:19 siem event=correlated severity=high

SECURITY OPERATIONS

Why Logging Matters

Logs turn technical activity into observable evidence. In security operations, they help establish what happened, when it happened, and which systems or identities were involved.

Detect security incidents

Identify suspicious behavior, failed authentication, unexpected changes, and other indicators of attack.

Support investigations

Reconstruct event sequences and correlate activity across systems, users, and devices.

Meet compliance requirements

Maintain records required for audits, internal policies, and regulatory requirements.

Monitor system health

Observe errors, availability, performance, and abnormal infrastructure behavior.

Provide forensic evidence

Preserve information that can support post-incident analysis and event reconstruction.
